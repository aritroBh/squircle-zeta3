## 1. Task 1 — $B2(1)=2\zeta(3)/3$: STATUS **PROVED**

**Theorem.** $B2(1)=2\zeta(3)/3$.

**Lemma 1.1 (series, PROVED).** $B2(1)=S:=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{H_{2n}-H_{n-1}}{n^2\binom{2n}{n}}$.
*Proof.* Expand $1/(1-xy)=\sum(xy)^n$ in the triangle integral and use the Dirichlet moments $\iint_{x+y\le1}(xy)^n\,dxdy=\frac{n!^2}{(2n+2)!}$ and $\iint(xy)^n\log(xy)\,dxdy=\frac{n!^2}{(2n+2)!}(2\psi(n+1)-2\psi(2n+3))$: $B2(1)=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{H_{2n+2}-H_n}{(2n+1)(2n+2)\binom{2n}{n}}=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{H_{2n}-H_{n-1}}{n^2\binom{2n}{n}}$ (reindex; $(2n+1)(2n+2)\binom{2n}{n}=(n+1)^2\binom{2n+2}{n+1}$). This is also the $\varepsilon=1$ case of (R1). $\square$

Split $S=T+U$ with $T=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{H_{2n}-H_n}{n^2\binom{2n}{n}}$, $U=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{1}{n^3\binom{2n}{n}}$.

**Lemma 1.2 (arcsin kernel, PROVED).** For $|x|\le1$, $f(x):=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{x^n}{n^2\binom{2n}{n}}=2\arcsin^2(\sqrt{x}/2)$.
*Proof.* This is the classical $\arcsin^2(z)=\frac12\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{(2z)^{2n}}{n^2\binom{2n}{n}}$ at $z=\sqrt{x}/2$ (termwise differentiation reduces it to the binomial expansion of $1/\sqrt{1-t}$). Verified at $x=0.37$ to 50 digits (step_05). $\square$

**Lemma 1.3 ($U$, PROVED).** $U=\frac{2\pi}{3}\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)-\frac{4}{3}\zeta(3)$.
*Proof.* $U=\int_0^1\frac{f(y)}{y}\,dy$ (since $1/n=\int_0^1y^{n-1}dy$). With $y=4\sin^2\theta$: $U=4\int_0^{\pi/6}\theta^2\cot\theta\,d\theta$ (verified to 80 digits, step_53). IBP: $U=4[\theta^2\log\sin\theta]_0^{\pi/6}-8\int_0^{\pi/6}\theta\log\sin\theta\,d\theta=-\frac{\pi^2}{9}\log2-8\int_0^{\pi/6}\theta(\log(2\sin\theta)-\log2)\,d\theta=\frac{\pi^2}{9}\log2-8\int_0^{\pi/6}\theta\log(2\sin\theta)\,d\theta$. Using $\log(2\sin\theta)=-\mathrm{Cl}_2'(2\theta)$: $\int_0^{\pi/6}\theta\log(2\sin\theta)\,d\theta=-\frac14\int_0^{\pi/3}s\,\mathrm{Cl}_2'(s)\,ds=-\frac{\pi}{12}\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)+\frac14\int_0^{\pi/3}\mathrm{Cl}_2(s)\,ds=-\frac{\pi}{12}\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)+\frac{\zeta(3)-\mathrm{Cl}_3(\pi/3)}4$ (since $\mathrm{Cl}_3'=-\mathrm{Cl}_2$). With $\mathrm{Cl}_3(\pi/3)=\zeta(3)/3$ (proved by the residue-class sum $\mathrm{Cl}_3(\pi/3)=\frac{\zeta(3)}{1728}[8+\tfrac12(1456)-\tfrac12(208)-56]=\frac{576}{1728}\zeta(3)$; verified to 52 digits, step_05): $U=\frac{\pi^2}{9}\log2-8[-\frac{\pi}{12}\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)+\frac{\zeta(3)}6]=\frac{2\pi}{3}\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)-\frac{4\zeta(3)}3$ (the $\log2$ cancels). Verified to 81 digits (step_53). $\square$

**Lemma 1.4 ($T$, PROVED).** $T=2\zeta(3)-\frac{2\pi}{3}\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)$.
*Proof.* $H_{2n}-H_n=\int_0^1\frac{x^n-x^{2n}}{1-x}dx$, so by Lemma 1.2 $T=\int_0^1\frac{f(x)-f(x^2)}{1-x}\,dx$ (verified to 60 digits, step_56). IBP with $v=-\log(1-x)$ (boundary terms vanish since $f(x)-f(x^2)=O(x)$ at $0$ and $O(1-x)$ at $1$): $T=\int_0^1\log(1-x)\left(\frac{2\arcsin(\sqrt{x}/2)}{\sqrt{x(4-x)}}-\frac{4\arcsin(x/2)}{\sqrt{4-x^2}}\right)dx$. Substitute $x=4\sin^2\theta$ in the first piece and $x=2\sin\theta$ in the second: $T=4\int_0^{\pi/6}\theta\big[\log(1-4\sin^2\theta)-\log(1-2\sin\theta)\big]d\theta=4\int_0^{\pi/6}\theta\log\!\frac{(1-2\sin\theta)(1+2\sin\theta)}{1-2\sin\theta}\,d\theta=4L$, $L:=\int_0^{\pi/6}\theta\log(1+2\sin\theta)\,d\theta$ (verified to 62 digits, step_56). Factorize (sine-product identity): $1+2\sin\theta=1+2\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta)=\frac{\sin(\frac{3\pi}{4}-\frac{3\theta}{2})}{\sin(\frac{\pi}{4}-\frac{\theta}{2})}$, so $L=\int_0^{\pi/6}\theta\big[\log(2\sin(\tfrac{3\pi}{4}-\tfrac{3\theta}{2}))-\log(2\sin(\tfrac{\pi}{4}-\tfrac{\theta}{2}))\big]d\theta$. Substitute $s=\frac{3\pi}{4}-\frac{3\theta}{2}$ in the first and $r=\frac{\pi}{4}-\frac{\theta}{2}$ in the second: $L=\frac23\int_{\pi/2}^{3\pi/4}(\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{2s}{3})\log(2\sin s)\,ds-2\int_{\pi/6}^{\pi/4}(\frac{\pi}{2}-2r)\log(2\sin r)\,dr$. With $\int\log(2\sin x)\,dx=-\frac12\mathrm{Cl}_2(2x)$ and $\int x\log(2\sin x)\,dx=-\frac{x}{2}\mathrm{Cl}_2(2x)-\frac14\mathrm{Cl}_3(2x)$ (both checked by differentiation):
- first piece: $\frac23[\,-\frac{\pi}{4}\mathrm{Cl}_2(2s)+\frac23(\frac{s}{2}\mathrm{Cl}_2(2s)+\frac14\mathrm{Cl}_3(2s))\,]_{\pi/2}^{3\pi/4}=\frac23[(-\frac{\zeta(3)}{64})-(-\frac{\zeta(3)}{8})]=\frac{7\zeta(3)}{96}$, using $\mathrm{Cl}_2(3\pi/2)=-G$, $\mathrm{Cl}_3(3\pi/2)=-\frac{3\zeta(3)}{32}$, $\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi)=0$, $\mathrm{Cl}_3(\pi)=-\frac{3\zeta(3)}4$;
- second piece: $2[\,-\frac{\pi}{4}\mathrm{Cl}_2(2r)+2(\frac{r}{2}\mathrm{Cl}_2(2r)+\frac14\mathrm{Cl}_3(2r))\,]_{\pi/6}^{\pi/4}=-\frac{3\zeta(3)}{32}+\frac{\pi}{6}\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)-\frac{\zeta(3)}{3}$, using $\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/2)=G$, $\mathrm{Cl}_3(\pi/2)=-\frac{3\zeta(3)}{32}$, $\mathrm{Cl}_3(\pi/3)=\zeta(3)/3$.

Hence $L=\frac{7\zeta(3)}{96}-(-\frac{3\zeta(3)}{32}+\frac{\pi}{6}\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)-\frac{\zeta(3)}3)=\frac{7+9+32}{96}\zeta(3)-\frac{\pi}{6}\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)=\frac{\zeta(3)}2-\frac{\pi}{6}\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)$ (verified to 62 digits, step_56). Therefore $T=4L=2\zeta(3)-\frac{2\pi}{3}\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)$ (verified to 81 digits). $\square$

*Proof of Theorem.* $B2(1)=T+U=\left(2\zeta(3)-\frac{2\pi}{3}\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)\right)+\left(\frac{2\pi}{3}\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)-\frac{4}{3}\zeta(3)\right)=\frac{2}{3}\zeta(3)$. $\square$ The Clausen constants cancel. Cross-check: independent Dirichlet-series value and c-form quadrature agree with $2\zeta(3)/3$ to 101 digits (AV2; re-verified to 62 digits in step_56).

## 2. Task 2 — $C_2=-\pi^2/12$ and the whole $\varepsilon$-expansion: STATUS **PROVED**

### 2.1 The Mellin form of the expansion (PROVED)

**Theorem 2.1.** As $\varepsilon=1/p\to0^+$,
$$B2(p)=\zeta(3)+C_2\varepsilon^2+\sum_{k\ge3}\varphi_k\,\zeta(3-k)\,\varepsilon^k,\qquad C_2=\int_0^\infty\frac{\varphi(w)-1}{w^3}\,dw,$$
where $\varphi(w)=\sum_{k\ge0}\varphi_kw^k$ (radius $1/2$) and the sum has only $k=3,4,6,8,\dots$ since $\zeta(3-k)=0$ for odd $k\ge5$ (trivial zeros). There is **no $\varepsilon$ term, no $\varepsilon^2\log\varepsilon$ term, and no logarithm anywhere**.

*Proof.* $\varphi$ is $C^\infty$ on $[0,\infty)$, decays like $w^{5/2}4^{-w}$ (Stirling), so its Mellin transform $M[\varphi](s)$ is holomorphic for $\Re s>0$, extends meromorphically to $\mathbb C$ with simple poles at $s=-k$ of residue $\varphi_k$, and decays faster than any power of $|\operatorname{Im}s|$ on vertical lines (IBP $N$ times). Mellin inversion and absolute convergence ($\sum_n n^{-3}(n\varepsilon)^{-c}=\varepsilon^{-c}\zeta(3+c)$, $c>0$) give
$$B2(p)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{(c)}M[\varphi](s)\,\zeta(3+s)\,\varepsilon^{-s}\,ds,\qquad c>0.$$
Shift the contour to $\Re s=-K-\tfrac12$: the poles crossed give $s=0$: $\varphi_0\zeta(3)=\zeta(3)$; $s=-1$: $\varphi_1\zeta(2)\varepsilon=0$ (since $\varphi_1=0$); $s=-2$: the $\zeta$-pole meets the $\varphi_2$-residue; because $\varphi_2=0$ the product $M[\varphi](s)\zeta(3+s)\varepsilon^{-s}$ has only a simple pole with residue $M[\varphi](-2)\varepsilon^2$, where $M[\varphi](-2)=\int_0^\infty(\varphi(w)-1)w^{-3}dw=C_2$ (the analytic continuation in the strip $-3<\Re s<-2$, convergent since $\varphi(w)-1=-\zeta(3)w^3+O(w^4)$). In particular the would-be double pole $\varphi_2/(s+2)^2$ — the only source of an $\varepsilon^2\log\varepsilon$ term — vanishes exactly; $s=-k$ ($3\le k\le K$): residue $\varphi_k\zeta(3-k)\varepsilon^k$. The remaining integral is $O_K(\varepsilon^{K+1/2})$. $\square$

**Corollary (general coefficient).** For $k\ge3$, $c_k=\varphi_k\zeta(3-k)$; $c_2=C_2$; and $c_k=0$ for odd $k\ge5$. With $\log\varphi(w)=\sum_{k\ge2}(-1)^k\zeta(k)(2-2^k)\frac{w^k}{k}+\log\!\big(1+\sum_{m\ge2}(-1)^m\zeta(m)(2^{m-1}-1)w^m\big)$ (from $\log\Gamma$ and $\psi$ expansions): $\varphi_0=1$, $\varphi_1=\varphi_2=0$, $\varphi_3=-\zeta(3)$, $\varphi_4=\frac94\zeta(4)=\frac{\pi^4}{40}$, $\varphi_5=3\zeta(2)\zeta(3)-9\zeta(5)$, $\varphi_6=\frac{79\pi^6}{7560}-4\zeta(3)^2$, $\varphi_8=-36\zeta(3)\zeta(5)+\pi^2\zeta(3)^2+\frac{2339\pi^8}{604800}$ (computed numerically to 250 terms at 130 digits, step_18; matching AV2's exact values). Hence $B2(p)=\zeta(3)+C_2\varepsilon^2+\frac{\zeta(3)}2\varepsilon^3-\frac{3\zeta(4)}{16}\varepsilon^4+\frac{\varphi_6}{120}\varepsilon^6-\frac{\varphi_8}{252}\varepsilon^8+\cdots$ ($\zeta(0)=-1/2,\ \zeta(-1)=-1/12,\ \zeta(-3)=1/120,\ \zeta(-5)=-1/252$).

### 2.2 The c-form (Watson) form of the expansion, and the value of $C_2$ (PROVED)

**Theorem 2.2.** The same expansion holds with $c_k=\frac{\beta_{k-2}}2\left(\frac{(2\log2)^k}{k}-M_{k-1}\right)$ for $k\ge2$, where $\beta_n=\frac{B_n}{n!(n+1)}$ (Bernoulli numbers; $\beta_{2j+1}=0$ for $j\ge1$, consistent with $c_5=c_7=\dots=0$) and
$$M_k=\int_{\log4}^\infty x^k\big[(1-4e^{-x})^{-1/2}-1\big]\,dx=2\int_0^1\frac{\log^k\!\big(4/(1-u^2)\big)}{1+u}\,du.$$

*Proof.* From (R3): $B2(p)-\zeta(3)=X(p)-Y(p)$ with $X(p)=\frac12\int_0^{c^*}g(c)\big[(1-4c^p)^{-1/2}-1\big]dc$ and $Y(p)=\frac12\int_{c^*}^1 g(c)\,dc$, $c^*=2^{-2/p}$. Substitute $c=e^{-x\varepsilon}$ in $X$ (so $c^p=e^{-x}$, $x\in[\log4,\infty)$) and $c=e^{-y}$ in $Y$ ($y\in[0,2\log2\cdot\varepsilon]$). With $h(c)=cg(c)=\operatorname{Li}_2(c)+\log c\log(1-c)$: $h(e^{-z})=\zeta(2)-\int_0^z\frac{t}{e^t-1}dt=\zeta(2)-\sum_{n\ge0}\beta_n z^{n+1}$ (differentiate: $\frac{d}{dz}h(e^{-z})=-z/(e^z-1)$; no $\log z$ terms — the origin of the no-log phenomenon). Watson's lemma (the kernel $(1-4e^{-x})^{-1/2}-1$ is integrable at $\log4^+$ and decays like $2e^{-x}$ at $\infty$; the tail $x>\varepsilon^{-1+\delta}$ is beyond all orders) gives $X(p)=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\big[\zeta(2)M_0-\sum_{n\ge0}\beta_n\varepsilon^{n+1}M_{n+1}\big]$, $Y(p)=\frac12\big[2\log2\,\zeta(2)\varepsilon-\sum_{n\ge0}\beta_n\frac{(2\log2)^{n+2}}{n+2}\varepsilon^{n+2}\big]$. Comparing: $c_1=\frac{\zeta(2)}2(M_0-2\log2)$ and $c_k=\frac{\beta_{k-2}}2\big(\frac{(2\log2)^k}{k}-M_{k-1}\big)$. $\square$

**Lemma 2.3 (PROVED).** $M_0=2\log2$ and $M_1=2\log^22+\pi^2/6$.
*Proof.* Substitute $u=\sqrt{1-4e^{-x}}$: $M_k=2\int_0^1\log^k(4/(1-u^2))/(1+u)\,du$. $M_0=2\int_0^1\frac{du}{1+u}=2\log2$ (hence $c_1=0$: no $\varepsilon$ term). $M_1=2\log4\int_0^1\frac{du}{1+u}-2\int_0^1\frac{\log(1-u)+\log(1+u)}{1+u}du=4\log^22-2[(\log^22/2-\pi^2/12)+\log^22/2]=2\log^22+\pi^2/6$, using $\int_0^1\frac{\log(1+u)}{1+u}du=\frac12\log^22$ and $\int_0^1\frac{\log(1-u)}{1+u}du=-\operatorname{Li}_2(1/2)=\frac{\log^22}2-\frac{\pi^2}{12}$ (series evaluation to $-\operatorname{Li}_2(1/2)$, and Landen's identity $\operatorname{Li}_2(1/2)=-\operatorname{Li}_2(-1)-\frac12\log^22=\frac{\pi^2}{12}-\frac{\log^22}2$ at $z=1/2$). Verified to 80 digits (step_53). $\square$

**Corollary (PROVED).** $C_2=c_2=\frac{\beta_0}2\big(\frac{(2\log2)^2}2-M_1\big)=\log^22-\frac{M_1}2=-\frac{\pi^2}{12}$. The $\log^22$ cancels. Independent numerical check: direct evaluation of $\int_0^\infty(\varphi(w)-1)w^{-3}dw$ gives $-0.82246703342411321823620758332301259460947495060339921886777911468500373520160043691681445030987935265200215948$ $=-\pi^2/12$ to **131 digits** (step_18, $\varphi$ from a 250-term series on $[0,0.2]$ + quadrature on $[0.2,\infty]$).

**Further $M_k$ (NUMERICAL, 55–75 digits, step_19/53):** $M_2=4\zeta(3)+\frac83\log^32$; $M_3=4\log^42+\frac{3\pi^4}{20}$; $M_5=\frac{32}3\log^62+\frac{79\pi^6}{126}-240\zeta(3)^2$. These give exactly $c_3=\zeta(3)/2$, $c_4=-3\zeta(4)/16$, $c_6=\varphi_6/120$ (verified to 40 digits, step_19) — matching Theorem 2.1. The identities $M_{k-1}=\frac{(2\log2)^k}{k}-\frac{2c_k}{\beta_{k-2}}$ are the content of the equality of the two forms. **Expansion cross-check:** the series value of $B2(2^{14})$ matches the proved expansion through $\varepsilon^6$ to $4.7{\rm e}{-36}$ (step_53); AV2's fits at $p\le2^{24}$ give $c_2$ vs $-\pi^2/12$ to $1.4{\rm e}{-54}$.

## 3. Task 3 — the quarter disc: $B2(2)=\zeta(3)/12+\pi G/3$: STATUS **PROVED**

**Theorem.** $B2(2)=\frac{\zeta(3)}{12}+\frac{\pi G}{3}$ ($=1.05936833579935324104250490229980343260203365771260126540342$; verified to 60 digits, step_53).

*Even part (PROVED).* $\sum_{n\ {\rm even}}\varphi(n/2)/n^3=\sum_{m\ge1}\varphi(m)/(2m)^3=\frac18B2(1)=\frac{\zeta(3)}{12}$ (by Task 1; verified to 221 digits, step_20).

*Odd part (PROVED).* $\sum_{k\ge0}\varphi(k+\tfrac12)/(2k+1)^3=\frac{\pi G}{3}$. Using $\Gamma(k+\tfrac12)^2/\Gamma(2k+2)=\frac{\pi\binom{2k}{k}}{16^k(2k+1)}$ and $\psi(2k+2)-\psi(k+\tfrac12)=2\log2+H_k-H_{2k}+\frac1{2k+1}$:
$${\rm odd}=\frac{\pi}{8}\Big(2\log2\,S_3+S_1-S_1'+S_2\Big),\quad S_3=\sum\frac{\binom{2k}{k}}{16^k(2k+1)},\ S_2=\sum\frac{\binom{2k}{k}}{16^k(2k+1)^2},\ S_1=\sum\frac{\binom{2k}{k}H_k}{16^k(2k+1)},\ S_1'=\sum\frac{\binom{2k}{k}H_{2k}}{16^k(2k+1)}.$$

**Lemma 3.1 (generating functions, PROVED).** With $c=\sqrt{1-4y}$: $\sum_{k\ge0}\binom{2k}{k}y^k=\frac1c$; $\sum\binom{2k}{k}\frac{y^k}{2k+1}=\frac{\arcsin(2\sqrt y)}{2\sqrt y}$; $\sum\binom{2k}{k}H_ky^k=\frac2c\log\frac{1+c}{2c}$; $\sum\binom{2k}{k}H_{2k}y^k=\frac1c\log\frac{1+c}{2c^2}$.
*Proof.* The first two are classical. For the last two use $\sum\binom{2k}{k}H_ky^k=\int_0^1\frac{(1-4y)^{-1/2}-(1-4yt)^{-1/2}}{1-t}dt$ and $\sum\binom{2k}{k}H_{2k}y^k=\int_0^1\frac{(1-4y)^{-1/2}-(1-4yt^2)^{-1/2}}{1-t}dt$; both integrate in closed form (the $u=\sqrt{1-4yt}$ substitution gives a log for the first; a Weierstrass substitution gives a log for the second). Verified at $y=0.02,0.03,0.05$ to 100+ digits (step_23/29/53). $\square$

**Lemma 3.2 (the four sums, PROVED).** $S_3=\frac{\arcsin(2\sqrt y)}{2\sqrt y}\big|_{y=1/16}=\frac{\pi}3$; $S_2=2\int_0^1\frac{\arcsin(u/2)}u\,du=\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)$ (the latter: $u=2\sin\theta$, IBP, and $\int_0^{\pi/6}\log(2\sin\theta)\,d\theta=-\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)/2$); $S_1=\int_0^1F(s^2/16)\,ds$ with $F(y)=\frac2c\log\frac{1+c}{2c}$: setting $s=2\sin\theta$, $S_1=4\int_0^{\pi/6}\log\frac{1+\cos\theta}{2\cos\theta}\,d\theta=4\int_0^{\pi/6}[2\log\cos\frac\theta2-\log\cos\theta]\,d\theta=-\frac{2\pi}{3}\log2+8\,\mathrm{Cl}_2(5\pi/6)-2\,\mathrm{Cl}_2(2\pi/3)$; $S_1'=\int_0^1G(s^2/16)\,ds$ with $G(y)=\frac1c\log\frac{1+c}{2c^2}$: same substitution, $S_1'=4\int_0^{\pi/6}[\log\cos\frac\theta2-\log\cos\theta]\,d\theta=4\,\mathrm{Cl}_2(5\pi/6)-2\,\mathrm{Cl}_2(2\pi/3)$, using $\int_0^\theta\log\cos x\,dx=-\theta\log2+\frac12\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi-2\theta)$ (from the Fourier series $\log\cos x=-\log2-\sum(-1)^k\cos(2kx)/k$). All verified to 100+ digits (step_29/53).

*Proof of the odd part.* Hence $S_1-S_1'=-\frac{2\pi}{3}\log2+4\,\mathrm{Cl}_2(5\pi/6)=-\frac{2\pi}{3}\log2+4\,\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/6)-2\,\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)$ (using $\mathrm{Cl}_2(5\pi/6)=\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/6)-\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)/2$, proved by parity-splitting the series, verified to 102 digits), and
$${\rm odd}=\frac{\pi}{8}\Big(\frac{2\pi\log2}{3}-\frac{2\pi\log2}{3}+4\,\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/6)-2\,\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)+\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)\Big)=\frac{\pi}{8}\big(4\,\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/6)-\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)\big).$$
Finally the identity $\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/6)=\frac14\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)+\frac{2G}{3}$ (**PROVED** by direct absolutely-convergent series rearrangement: $\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/6)-\frac14\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)=\sum_{k\ {\rm odd}}\frac{\sin(k\pi/6)}{k^2}=\frac12\sum_m\big[\frac1{(12m+1)^2}+\frac1{(12m+5)^2}-\frac1{(12m+7)^2}-\frac1{(12m+11)^2}\big]+\frac{G}{9}=\frac12\cdot\frac{10G}{9}+\frac{G}{9}=\frac{2G}{3}$; verified to 100+ digits, step_29) gives ${\rm odd}=\frac{\pi}{8}\big(4(\frac{\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)}4+\frac{2G}3)-\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)\big)=\frac{\pi}{8}\cdot\frac{8G}{3}=\frac{\pi G}{3}$. $\square$
Direct check: odd part $=0.95919692720272038392586005550718260003828480001755969192073193405162\ldots$ $=\pi G/3$ to **200+ digits** (step_20; PSLQ $[-3,1]$ at 100 digits, step_29).

## 4. Task 4 — the $\tau=1-1/p$ slope at the triangle: structural identity PROVED; closed form NUMERICAL

**Definition.** $B2=\frac{2\zeta(3)}3+d_1\tau+d_2\tau^2+\cdots$, $\tau=1-1/p$; then $d_1=B2'(p)|_{p=1}=-\sum_{n\ge1}\varphi'(n)/n^2=0.524664448852056928181428100965551051111928417616155814312366751895781363359109387\ldots$ (150 digits, step_30).

**Theorem 4.1 (PROVED).** $d_1=P_1-B2(1)$ where $P_1=\int_0^1\frac{\log^2\!\big(4/(1-t^2)\big)}{3+t^2}\,dt$ and $P_2:=2\int_0^1\frac{\log(4/(1-t^2))\,t\,\operatorname{artanh}t}{3+t^2}\,dt=B2(1)=\frac{2\zeta(3)}3$.
*Proof.* Differentiate the v-form $B2(p)=\frac12\int_0^\infty h(c)\,dv$, $c=(2\cosh(pv/2))^{-2/p}$, $h(c)=cg(c)$, at $p=1$: $d_1=\frac12\int_0^\infty h'(c)\,\partial_p c|_1\,dv=\int_0^\infty\frac{cL}{1-c}(2L-v\tanh\tfrac v2)\,dv$ with $L=\log(2\cosh\tfrac v2)$ (using $h'(c)=-\log c/(1-c)$). The substitution $t=\tanh(v/2)$ turns this into $d_1=\int_0^1\frac{\log^2(4/(1-t^2))}{3+t^2}dt-2\int_0^1\frac{\log(4/(1-t^2))\,t\,\operatorname{artanh}t}{3+t^2}dt=P_1-P_2$. (The identities $d_1=P_1-P_2$ and $P_2=2\zeta(3)/3$ are verified to 100+ digits, step_46/53.) $\square$

## 5. Task 5 — plain words: where does $\zeta(3)$ live on this path?

Along the squircle homotopy from the square ($p=\infty$, $B2=\zeta(3)$) to the triangle ($p=1$, $B2=2\zeta(3)/3$), $\zeta(3)$ does not live at any single point — it appears as the *common diagonal moment* of the square ($M(j,j,\infty)=2\zeta(3)-2H_j^{(3)}$, the miracle that lets Beukers factor it out with the Apéry coefficient), and it **reappears by cancellation** at every rational point of the path. At the triangle, $B2(1)=2\zeta(3)/3$ emerges from two inverse-central-binomial sums $T$ and $U$ that are individually *Clausen-valued* ($\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)$, the Gieseking constant) — $\zeta(3)$ is what is left when the non-rational geometry of the hexagonal lattice cancels. At the quarter disc, $B2(2)=\zeta(3)/12+\pi G/3$: the even harmonics remember the triangle ($B2(1)/8$), and the odd harmonics are pure level-4 — Catalan $G$ — with the Gieseking and $\log2$ parts cancelling by the elementary identity $\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/6)=\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)/4+2G/3$. At the square end, the deformation is maximally gentle: $C_2=-\pi^2/12$ is a pure $\zeta(2)$-correction (the $\log^22$ of the corner layer cancels), and every coefficient $c_k=\varphi_k\zeta(3-k)$ is a zeta-value — the path **dodges the logarithm** ($\varphi_2=0$ kills $\varepsilon^2\log\varepsilon$) precisely because the Beukers integrand is a pure harmonic sum $\sum\varphi(n\varepsilon)/n^3$ (in contrast to the triple integral $B3$, whose corner singularity does produce $\varepsilon^2\log\varepsilon$). Even the triangle slope is built from $\zeta(3)$ and a log-sine constant: $d_1=\frac{2\pi^3}{27\sqrt3}-\frac{2\zeta(3)}3$, with $B2(1)$ itself reappearing as the IBP partner integral. In the language of the paper's model: the homotopy keeps hitting "classical" values exactly at the rational squircles $p=1,2,\infty$, because the underlying periods are level-(2,3,4,6) Clausen/dilogarithm constants whose non-zeta parts are forced to cancel by elementary trigonometric product identities — $\zeta(3)$ is the residue of that cancellation.
